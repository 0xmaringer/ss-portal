1. Newton's First Law & The Principle of Inertia

Newton's First Law of Motion: Every body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external net force[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).

Inertia is the inherent reluctance of a physical body to change its state of rest or uniform motion[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span). Mass is the quantitative measure of inertia[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span)[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span). A fully loaded haulage truck possesses greater inertia than a passenger car; thus, it requires a much larger opposing force to arrest its motion over an identical duration[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).

Worked Example 1.1 Threshold Friction

A metal crate of mass \(15\text{ kg}\) rests on a rough horizontal depot floor where the limiting static friction is \(45\text{ N}\)[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span). If a worker applies a horizontal pull of \(30\text{ N}\), calculate the acceleration produced[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).

$$\text{Applied Pull } P = 30\text{ N}$$ $$\text{Limiting Static Friction } F_s = 45\text{ N}$$ $$\text{Since } P < F_s \text{, the applied force cannot overcome static friction[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).}$$ $$\text{Net Unbalanced Force } F_{net} = 0\text{ N}$$ $$a = \frac{F_{net}}{m} = \frac{0}{15} = 0\text{ m s}^{-2}$$

2. Linear Momentum, Impulse & Newton's Second Law

Linear Momentum (\(p\)) is the product of mass and velocity[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span):

$$p = m \cdot v \quad [\text{Unit: kg m s}^{-1} \text{ or N s}] \text{[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span)}$$
Newton's Second Law of Motion: The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the straight line in which the force acts[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).

From this definition, force is derived mathematically[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span):

$$F \propto \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t}$$ $$\text{Since acceleration } a = \frac{v - u}{t} \text{[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span)}:$$ $$F = m \cdot a \text{[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span)}$$

Impulse (\(I\)) is the product of an applied force and the duration of its impact[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span)[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span):

$$I = F \cdot \Delta t = m(v - u) = \Delta p \text{[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span)[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)}$$

Worked Example 2.1 WAEC Standard

A wooden block of mass \(5\text{ kg}\) is pulled on a platform by a force of \(40\text{ N}\)[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span). If the frictional force experienced by the block is \(12\text{ N}\), calculate the magnitude of the acceleration produced[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).

$$\text{Mass of block } m = 5\text{ kg}$$ $$\text{Applied Force } P = 40\text{ N}$$ $$\text{Opposing Frictional Force } F_f = 12\text{ N}$$ $$\text{Resultant Unbalanced Force } F_r = P - F_f \text{[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span)}$$ $$F_r = 40 - 12 = 28\text{ N}$$ $$\text{From } F_r = m \cdot a \text{[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)}:$$ $$a = \frac{F_r}{m} = \frac{28}{5} = 5.6\text{ m s}^{-2} \text{[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span)}$$

Worked Example 2.2 Impulse Vectoring

A \(0.2\text{ kg}\) tennis ball moving horizontally at \(30\text{ m s}^{-1}\) is struck by a racket, returning along its initial path at \(40\text{ m s}^{-1}\). If the impact lasted for \(0.005\text{ s}\), calculate: (i) the impulse, (ii) the average contact force.

$$\text{Let initial direction be positive: } u = +30\text{ m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Reversed final velocity: } v = -40\text{ m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{(i) Impulse } I = m(v - u)$$ $$I = 0.2 \times \left(-40 - 30\right) = 0.2 \times (-70) = -14\text{ N s}$$ $$\text{Magnitude of Impulse } = 14\text{ N s}$$ $$\text{(ii) Average Force } F = \frac{I}{\Delta t} = \frac{-14}{0.005} = -2800\text{ N}$$ $$\text{Magnitude of Force } = 2800\text{ N}$$

3. Apparent Weight in a Lift System & Inertial Mass

When a person of true mass \(m\) stands on a weighing scale inside a lift (elevator), the scale reads the normal reaction \(R\) exerted on their feet[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span).

Motion State of Lift Governing Equation Apparent Weight (\(R\))
Stationary or Uniform Velocity (\(a = 0\)) \(R - mg = 0\) \(R = mg\) (True Weight)
Accelerating Upwards with \(a\) \(R - mg = ma\) \(R = m(g + a)\) (Heavier)[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span)
Accelerating Downwards with \(a\) \(mg - R = ma\) \(R = m(g - a)\) (Lighter)[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span)
Free Fall (Cable Snaps, \(a = g\)) \(mg - R = mg\) \(R = 0\) (Weightlessness)[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span)

Worked Example 3.1 Elevator Mechanics

An engineer of mass \(75\text{ kg}\) stands on a compression balance inside an elevator cabin. Taking \(g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}\), calculate the balance reading when the cabin accelerates: (i) upwards at \(2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}\), (ii) downwards at \(3.0\text{ m s}^{-2}\)[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span).

$$\text{(i) Upward Acceleration:}$$ $$R = m(g + a) = 75 \times (10 + 2.5) = 75 \times 12.5 = 937.5\text{ N} \text{[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span)}$$ $$\text{(ii) Downward Acceleration:}$$ $$R = m(g - a) = 75 \times (10 - 3.0) = 75 \times 7.0 = 525.0\text{ N} \text{[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span)}$$

4. Newton's Third Law, Momentum Conservation & Collisions

Newton's Third Law of Motion: To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span)[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span).

Principle of Conservation of Linear Momentum: In an isolated system of colliding bodies, the total momentum before impact is strictly equal to the total momentum after impact, provided no external forces act[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span).

Worked Example 4.1 Inelastic Recoil

A lead bullet of mass \(20\text{ g}\) is discharged horizontally at \(400\text{ m s}^{-1}\) into a suspended ballistic timber block of mass \(1.98\text{ kg}\). If the bullet embeds itself into the block, determine their combined speed immediately after impact.

$$\text{Bullet Mass } m_1 = \frac{20}{1000}\text{ kg} = 0.02\text{ kg}, \quad u_1 = 400\text{ m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Block Mass } m_2 = 1.98\text{ kg}, \quad u_2 = 0\text{ m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Momentum before Impact } = m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2$$ $$= (0.02 \times 400) + (1.98 \times 0) = 8.0\text{ kg m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Momentum after Impact } = (m_1 + m_2)v = (0.02 + 1.98)v = 2.0 \cdot v$$ $$\text{By Momentum Conservation:}$$ $$2.0 \cdot v = 8.0 \implies v = \frac{8.0}{2.0} = 4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}$$

Worked Example 4.2 Pulley Assembly Equilibrium

Two bodies \(X\) and \(Y\) of masses \(5.0\text{ kg}\) and \(7.5\text{ kg}\) are coupled by a light inextensible cord passing over a frictionless pulley[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span). Body \(X\) sits on a rough bench with \(\mu = 0.5\)[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span). Taking \(g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}\), calculate the static tension in the string[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span).

$$\text{Weight of hanging mass } Y: W_y = m_y \cdot g = 7.5 \times 10 = 75\text{ N} \text{[span_47](start_span)[span_47](end_span)}$$ $$\text{Limiting friction on } X: F_f = \mu \cdot m_x \cdot g = 0.5 \times (5.0 \times 10) = 25\text{ N} \text{[span_48](start_span)[span_48](end_span)}$$ $$\text{For the stationary hanging load, tension balances weight: } T = W_y = 75\text{ N} \text{[span_49](start_span)[span_49](end_span)}$$

$$F_{net} = m \cdot a = 800 \times 2 = 1600\text{ N} \text{[span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span)}$$ $$\text{Applied Engine Force } P = F_{net} + F_{friction} = 1600 + 200 = 1800\text{ N} \text{[span_53](start_span)[span_53](end_span)}$$
Practice Drill 2: Rifle Recoil Action

A target rifle of mass \(4.5\text{ kg}\) discharges a \(0.03\text{ kg}\) cartridge at a muzzle velocity of \(600\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Compute the initial recoil velocity imparted to the rifle stock.

$$\text{Total initial momentum of system at rest } = 0$$ $$m_{rifle} \cdot v_{rifle} + m_{bullet} \cdot v_{bullet} = 0$$ $$(4.5) \cdot v_{rifle} + (0.03 \times 600) = 0$$ $$4.5 \cdot v_{rifle} + 18 = 0 \implies v_{rifle} = -\frac{18}{4.5} = -4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}$$ $$\text{Recoil velocity is } 4.0\text{ m s}^{-1} \text{ in the backward direction.}$$
