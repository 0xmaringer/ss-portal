1. Newton's First Law & Inertia

Newton's First Law of Motion: Every object continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless acted upon by an external resultant force.

Inertia is the natural tendency of a body to resist any change to its state of rest or uniform motion. The mass of an object is a quantitative measure of its inertia. A heavy lorry requires a much larger braking force than a small bicycle because its larger mass gives it much greater inertia.

  • Inertia of Rest: When a stationary bus suddenly surges forward, standing passengers jerk backward because their bodies tend to remain at rest relative to the ground.
  • Inertia of Motion: When a fast-moving bus brakes suddenly, passengers lurch forward because their bodies tend to continue moving forward at the original speed.

Worked Example 1.1 Static Friction Threshold

A metal crate of mass \(15\text{ kg}\) rests on a rough horizontal floor where the limiting static friction is \(45\text{ N}\). If a worker applies a horizontal pull of \(30\text{ N}\), calculate the acceleration of the crate.

Step 1: Write down the given forces \[P = 30\text{ N}, \quad F_s = 45\text{ N}, \quad m = 15\text{ kg}\]
Step 2: Compare applied pull with limiting static friction

Since the applied pull (\(30\text{ N}\)) is less than the limiting static friction (\(45\text{ N}\)), the crate does not move. Static friction simply matches the \(30\text{ N}\) pull.

\[F_r = 30\text{ N} - 30\text{ N} = 0\text{ N}\]
Step 3: Calculate acceleration \[a = \frac{F_r}{m} = \frac{0}{15} = 0\text{ m s}^{-2}\]

2. Momentum, Impulse & Newton's Second Law

Linear Momentum (\(p\)) of a body is defined as the product of its mass and its velocity:

\[p = m \times v \quad \text{(Unit: }\text{kg m s}^{-1}\text{ or }\text{N s}\text{)}\]
Newton's Second Law of Motion: The time rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts.

If a force \(F\) acts on a body of mass \(m\) for time \(t\) and changes its velocity from initial velocity \(u\) to final velocity \(v\):

\[F \propto \frac{mv - mu}{t} \implies F = \frac{m(v - u)}{t}\] \[\text{Since } a = \frac{v - u}{t}, \quad F = ma\]

Impulse (\(I\)) is the product of a force and the time for which it acts. It is equal to the total change in momentum:

\[I = F \times t = mv - mu = \Delta p \quad \text{(Unit: }\text{N s}\text{)}\]

Worked Example 2.1 Net Force & Friction

A block of wood of mass \(5\text{ kg}\) is pulled on a horizontal platform by a force of \(40\text{ N}\). If the frictional force \(F\) experienced by the block is \(12\text{ N}\), calculate the magnitude of the acceleration of the block.

Step 1: Find the resultant (net) force \(F_r\) \[F_r = P - F\] \[F_r = 40\text{ N} - 12\text{ N} = 28\text{ N}\]
Step 2: Apply Newton's Second Law (\(F_r = ma\)) \[a = \frac{F_r}{m}\] \[a = \frac{28}{5} = 5.6\text{ m s}^{-2}\]

Worked Example 2.2 Impulse & Rebound

A \(0.2\text{ kg}\) ball moving horizontally at \(30\text{ m s}^{-1}\) is hit back in the opposite direction at \(40\text{ m s}^{-1}\). If the contact time is \(0.005\text{ s}\), find: (i) the impulse, (ii) the average force exerted.

Step 1: Assign signs to directions (initial direction = positive) \[u = +30\text{ m s}^{-1}, \quad v = -40\text{ m s}^{-1}, \quad m = 0.2\text{ kg}\]
Step 2: Calculate Impulse \(I\) \[I = m(v - u) = 0.2(-40 - 30)\] \[I = 0.2 \times (-70) = -14\text{ N s} \quad (\text{Magnitude } = 14\text{ N s})\]
Step 3: Calculate Average Force \(F\) \[F = \frac{I}{t} = \frac{14}{0.005} = 2800\text{ N}\]

3. Weight of a Body in a Lift (Elevator) & Connected Pulleys

When a person of mass \(m\) stands on a spring scale inside a lift, the scale measures the upward normal reaction \(R\) of the floor on the person:

Lift Motion Equation of Motion Scale Reading (\(R\))
At rest or uniform speed (\(a = 0\)) \(R - mg = 0\) \(R = mg\) (True weight)
Accelerating upwards (\(a\)) \(R - mg = ma\) \(R = m(g + a)\) (Feels heavier)
Accelerating downwards (\(a\)) \(mg - R = ma\) \(R = m(g - a)\) (Feels lighter)
Free fall (Cable snaps, \(a = g\)) \(mg - R = mg\) \(R = 0\) (Weightlessness)

Worked Example 3.1 Apparent Weight in Lift

A man of mass \(75\text{ kg}\) stands on a spring scale inside a lift. Taking \(g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}\), find the scale reading when the lift accelerates: (i) upwards at \(2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}\), (ii) downwards at \(3.0\text{ m s}^{-2}\).

(i) Upward acceleration (\(a = 2.5\text{ m s}^{-2}\)) \[R = m(g + a) = 75(10 + 2.5)\] \[R = 75 \times 12.5 = 937.5\text{ N}\]
(ii) Downward acceleration (\(a = 3.0\text{ m s}^{-2}\)) \[R = m(g - a) = 75(10 - 3.0)\] \[R = 75 \times 7.0 = 525.0\text{ N}\]

Worked Example 3.2 Connected Pulley System

Two bodies \(X\) and \(Y\) of masses \(5.0\text{ kg}\) and \(7.5\text{ kg}\) are connected by a light inextensible string as shown in Diagram 3.1. If \(X\) is placed on a rough surface of coefficient of friction \(0.5\) and \(g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}\), calculate the magnitude of the: (i) normal reaction on \(X\), (ii) frictional force between \(X\) and the surface, (iii) static tension in the string.

(i) Normal reaction \(R_x\) on body \(X\) \[R_x = W_x = m_x g = 5.0 \times 10 = 50\text{ N}\]
(ii) Frictional force \(F\) opposing motion \[F = \mu R_x = 0.5 \times 50 = 25\text{ N}\]
(iii) Tension \(T\) in the string \[T = W_y = m_y g = 7.5 \times 10 = 75\text{ N}\]

4. Newton's Third Law & Conservation of Linear Momentum

Newton's Third Law: Action and reaction are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
Principle of Conservation of Linear Momentum: In any closed or isolated system of colliding bodies, the total linear momentum before collision is equal to the total linear momentum after collision, provided no external force acts on the system.
Elastic Collision Inelastic Collision
Bodies separate and bounce off after impact. Bodies stick together and move with a common velocity \(v\).
Both linear momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy decreases (converted to heat and sound).
\(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2\) \(m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v\)

Worked Example 4.1 Inelastic Collision

A bullet of mass \(20\text{ g}\) moving horizontally at \(400\text{ m s}^{-1}\) strikes a stationary wooden block of mass \(1.98\text{ kg}\) and embeds itself inside it. Find their common velocity immediately after impact.

Step 1: Convert all masses to kilograms \[m_1 = \frac{20}{1000} = 0.02\text{ kg}, \quad u_1 = 400\text{ m s}^{-1}\] \[m_2 = 1.98\text{ kg}, \quad u_2 = 0\text{ m s}^{-1}\]
Step 2: Apply Conservation of Momentum \[m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v\] \[(0.02 \times 400) + (1.98 \times 0) = (0.02 + 1.98)v\] \[8.0 = 2.0v \implies v = \frac{8.0}{2.0} = 4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}\]

Worked Example 4.2 Rocket Thrust

A rocket expels hot exhaust gases at a rate of \(40\text{ kg s}^{-1}\) with a downward velocity of \(500\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Calculate the upward thrust force on the rocket.

Step 1: Apply the mass-flow thrust formula \[F = v \times \frac{\Delta m}{\Delta t}\]
Step 2: Substitute given values \[F = 500 \times 40 = 20000\text{ N}\]

Practice Question 1

A moving car of mass \(800\text{ kg}\) experiences a frictional force of \(200\text{ N}\). If it accelerates at \(2\text{ m s}^{-2}\), calculate the magnitude of the driving force applied by the engine (in \(\text{N}\)).

Step 1: Calculate net resultant force \(F_r\) \[F_r = ma = 800 \times 2 = 1600\text{ N}\]
Step 2: Calculate applied engine force \(P\) \[P = F_r + F = 1600 + 200 = 1800\text{ N}\]
Practice Question 2

A gun of mass \(4.5\text{ kg}\) fires a bullet of mass \(30\text{ g}\) at a muzzle velocity of \(600\text{ m s}^{-1}\). Calculate the recoil speed of the gun (in \(\text{m s}^{-1}\)).

Step 1: Momentum before firing = Momentum after firing \[m_g v_g = m_b v_b\]
Step 2: Solve for recoil speed \(v_g\) \[4.5 \times v_g = 0.03 \times 600\] \[4.5 v_g = 18 \implies v_g = \frac{18}{4.5} = 4.0\text{ m s}^{-1}\]
Practice Question 3

A body of mass \(25\text{ kg}\) moving at \(3\text{ m s}^{-1}\) on a rough horizontal floor is brought to rest after sliding through \(2.5\text{ m}\). Taking \(g = 10\text{ m s}^{-2}\), calculate the coefficient of sliding friction \(\mu\).

Step 1: Find retardation \(a\) from \(v^2 = u^2 - 2as\) \[0 = 3^2 - 2(a)(2.5) \implies 5a = 9 \implies a = \frac{9}{5} = 1.8\text{ m s}^{-2}\]
Step 2: Find frictional force \(F\) and coefficient \(\mu\) \[F = ma = 25 \times 1.8 = 45\text{ N}\] \[R = mg = 25 \times 10 = 250\text{ N}\] \[\mu = \frac{F}{R} = \frac{45}{250} = 0.18\]
